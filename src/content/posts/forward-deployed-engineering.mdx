People often describe forward deployed engineering as consulting with a different title. I can see why, especially when [discussions about the role](https://www.reddit.com/r/cscareerquestions/comments/1wgf8yu/is_forward_deployed_engineer_just_a_rebranding_of/) include people doing very different jobs under the same name. There is plenty of overlap in the customer work, but the description leaves out much of what I find interesting about being an FDE.

In my experience, the role is about working closely with customers and product teams to build at the edge of what the product can do. You are helping a customer solve a business problem while learning how the product itself could become more useful. That combination of engineering, customer work, and zero-to-one building is what draws me to it.

## Build with the customer

To build something useful, I need to understand how the customer works. Spending time with the people who will use the product gives me context I would miss from a feature request alone, including why the problem matters and what a successful solution would look like in their day-to-day work.

The customer brings knowledge of their business, and I bring an understanding of the technology and what we can build with it. Working together helps us find an approach that makes sense for both. The product team is part of that conversation too, especially when the work starts to reach beyond what the product already supports.

Suppose a support team wants an assistant to answer policy questions. Working through actual questions together might reveal that each answer needs to identify the policy version and show the passage it relies on, so a specialist can check it before replying. That gives us a clearer idea of what to build and a practical way to judge whether it helps.

## Get something working and improve it together

A lot of the learning happens once the customer has something to try. Building that first version may involve connecting data, giving an agent access to tools, or fitting a new interface into an existing system. I want to get enough of the workflow working that we can use it together and see where it needs improvement.

In the support example, the team might find that the assistant gives accurate answers but makes the supporting documents difficult to open. We can then work on that specific problem and see whether the next version makes answers easier to review. Each iteration gives us a better understanding of what the customer needs and how to deliver it.

This is what I enjoy about zero-to-one work. You are developing the solution with the customer as you build, using their feedback to make engineering decisions and your technical judgment to explore what is possible.

## Help shape the product

Working on a deployment also gives you a detailed view of where the product could improve. When a customer needs something it cannot yet do, you can bring the product team a working example of the problem, explain what you tried, and show where the current approach falls short.

That kind of evidence helps the team decide what belongs in the product. Some needs are specific to one customer's systems, while others point to a capability that could help many customers. If several teams struggle to distinguish current policies from old ones, for example, better support for document versions may be worth building into the shared product.

I see this relationship as an important part of the role. The deployment benefits from the product team's work, and the product team learns from what happens during the deployment. Over time, that can expand what the product is capable of and make it easier for other customers to get value from it.

## Make the work valuable for both sides

For the customer, success should show up in the work they care about. In our support example, I would want to know whether specialists can respond sooner and spend less time checking or correcting answers. Those outcomes give us a better sense of the value than a demo on its own.

The company building the product should benefit too, through a successful customer relationship and a better understanding of where the product can grow. When an engagement helps a customer today and informs a capability that others can use tomorrow, the work has value well beyond that first deployment.

That is the kind of forward deployed engineering I want to do. I like being close to customers, building with them, and working with the product team to turn what we learn into something more people can use.
